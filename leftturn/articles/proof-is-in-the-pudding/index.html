The Proof is in the Pudding: A Few Comments on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics

Gary Olson

Fall / Winter 2025-2026 Vol. 6, No. 1–2 Pages 84–89 1 min read
Text size
Republished. This piece first appeared on 21 December 2025 in Dissident Voice, and is reproduced here under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

Metadata record. The full text of this piece has not yet been keyed in for the web edition. The opening is reproduced below; read the complete article in the print edition at pages84–89.

The Communist Party of China was founded in Shanghai in July 1921. At its inception the CPC had only 50 members — 100 million today — and came to power in 1949 after 28 years of revolutionary struggle.

The ideal of “Common Prosperity” had its origins in the CPC’s founding and was made official policy in December 1953 in a report drafted by Mao Zedong. It is important to clarify that the term envisioned an egalitarian, mutual aid type society in which resources would be held in common.

China incorporated market mechanisms, but they operate under the socialist state, which is controlled by the CPC, not private capitalists as is the case in the United States. Yes, millionaires and billionaires exist in China — some are even party members — but the CPC does not allow them to constitute a capitalist class. They’re prevented from establishing their own political parties or organizing their own media.
